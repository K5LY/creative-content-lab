# Storytelling Structures

Five narrative structures for brand and editorial content. Each comes with the job it does best and the failure mode to watch for.

---

## 1 · The Contrast Open

**Shape:** Two states of the world, side by side. Before / after, myth / reality, cost / value.

**Best for:** Launches, positioning pieces, campaign openers.

**Failure mode:** Contrast without stakes. If neither state costs the reader anything, the structure is decoration.

## 2 · The Process Story

**Shape:** The reader follows a real process, including the messy middle. Attempt → obstacle → adjustment → result.

**Best for:** Building credibility and showing craft. Case studies, build logs, "how we made this."

**Failure mode:** Skipping the obstacle. A process story with no friction reads as advertising.

## 3 · The Question Ladder

**Shape:** Open with the question the audience actually asks, answer it, then surface the deeper question the answer creates. Repeat.

**Best for:** Explainers, educational series, thought leadership.

**Failure mode:** Laddering to nowhere. Each question must get *more specific*, not more abstract.

## 4 · The Object Frame

**Shape:** Start from a single concrete object or moment, expand to the system around it.

**Best for:** Brand films, essays, giving big ideas a human entry point.

**Failure mode:** Object as ornament. If the opening object is dropped after paragraph one, readers feel cheated.

## 5 · The Reversal

**Shape:** Set up the expected narrative, then replace the premise with evidence the reader was looking at it backwards.

**Best for:** Attention-critical channels, challenging category assumptions.

**Failure mode:** Reversal without proof. A contrarian claim needs its strongest evidence *immediately* after the turn.

---

## Choosing one

| You need... | Use |
|---|---|
| A strong first impression | Contrast Open |
| Trust and credibility | Process Story |
| To teach something layered | Question Ladder |
| Warmth and memorability | Object Frame |
| To earn attention fast | Reversal |
